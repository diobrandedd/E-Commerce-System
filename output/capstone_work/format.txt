[44] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] The Background of the Study (found in Chapter I: Introduction) follows a four-paragraph flow that moves from the general topic, to the specific setting, to the problem, and finally to your proposed solution. This structure makes the logic of 'why this study exists' clear and easy to follow.
[45] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000'] Overall Structure (on this part the background of the study should have 2-3 page)
[46] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Paragraph 1 — Brief description of the study.
[47] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Paragraph 2 — The company/establishment and the process(es) they currently do.
[48] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Paragraph 3 — The problem(s) they encountered in that process.
[49] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Paragraph 4 — The solution, based on the features of your proposed system.
[50] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Paragraph 1: Brief Description of the Study
[51] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Open with a short overview of the project itself — this orients the reader before you introduce the setting. Keep it to 2–4 sentences and include:
[52] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] The full title of the study.
[53] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] The type of system/project it is (e.g., web-based, QR-code-based, mobile app).
[54] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] The institution or beneficiary it was made for.
[55] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] The core purpose it serves — in one sentence.
[56] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000'] TIP: Keep this brief — it's a preview, not a summary of the whole chapter.

[58] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Paragraph 2: The Company/Establishment and Its Process
[59] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Introduce the institution where the study was conducted. Describe what it does and, specifically, how it currently carries out the process your system will affect (e.g., how attendance is recorded, how payroll is computed).
[60] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Brief background of the establishment: what it is, its purpose, its scale (e.g., number of employees, years in operation).
[61] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] A step-by-step description of their CURRENT process — how things are done today, before your system.
[62] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Keep this factual and neutral; save judgment/criticism for Paragraph 3.
[63] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000'] TIP: This paragraph is often built from your interview or observation data — describe the actual workflow you observed, not an assumed one.

[66] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Now shift to the weaknesses of that current process. This paragraph should make the reader feel the problem is real and worth solving.
[67] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Identify specific issues: errors, delays, inefficiency, security risks, lost records, tampering, etc.
[68] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] If possible, support the claim with data from your interviews/questionnaires (e.g., 'X out of Y respondents reported...').
[69] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] End by stating the consequence of the problem (e.g., delayed payroll release, employee complaints, administrative burden).
[70] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000'] Use (1),(2) to easily to identify the problem 
[71] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000'] TIP: Every problem you state here should be one your system will actually solve in Paragraph 4 — don't introduce an issue your system doesn't address.
[72] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000'] "(1) This manual process, however, has resulted in recurring issues for the institution. [78] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000'] TIP: Use a clear 'problem → feature' pairing so the reader immediately sees the cause-and-effect, e.g., 'To address [problem], the system provides [feature].'

[94] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] GUIDE: WRITING THE OBJECTIVES OF THE STUDY
[95] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] General Objective and Specific Objectives
[96] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] The Objectives of the Study state exactly what your project intends to accomplish. They are split into one General Objective (the overall purpose) and several Specific Objectives (the concrete steps that achieve it). Together, they act as a checklist that Chapter IV's Conclusion must answer point by point.
[97] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] How the Two Relate
[98] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Think of the General Objective as the destination and the Specific Objectives as the road that gets you there. Every specific objective should contribute directly to achieving the general objective — nothing more, nothing less.
[99] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] A. General Objective
[100] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] A single, broad sentence that summarizes the overall purpose of the study. It is usually written first and answers the question: 'What is this study meant to achieve, in one sentence?'
[101] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Start with a fixed phrase such as 'This study aims to...' or 'This project seeks to...'
[102] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Name the system/project and its main function.
[103] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Name the institution/beneficiary it serves.
[104] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Keep it to one sentence — save the details for the specific objectives.
[105] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000'] TIP: If your General Objective sentence is more than 3 lines long, it is probably trying to do the job of a Specific Objective — trim it down.
[106] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] "This study aims to design and develop a Payroll, Employee Management, and Daily Time Record System Using QR Code for Dr. Francisco C. Parcon Clinic and Hospital to automate employee attendance monitoring and payroll processing."
[107] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] B. Specific Objectives
[108] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] A numbered list that breaks the General Objective down into concrete, achievable, and measurable actions. Each one should be something you can later point to and say 'yes, this was accomplished' in your Conclusion.
[109] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Start each item with a strong action verb (see verb table below) — never with vague verbs like 'know' or 'understand.'
[110] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Each objective should describe ONE clear action or deliverable — don't combine two goals with 'and' into a single item.
[111] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Arrange them in logical order — usually the order the system/process is built or used (e.g., design → develop → test → evaluate).
[112] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Keep the number of specific objectives manageable — typically 3 to 6 items.
[113] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Make sure each one is realistically achievable within your project's scope and timeline.
[114] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000'] TIP: A useful test: could a panel member ask 'did you achieve this?' and get a clear yes/no answer just by reading your objective? If not, it's too vague.
[115] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] "Specifically, this study aims to: 1. Design a database to securely store employee information, attendance records, and payroll data. 2. Develop a QR code-based time-in and time-out feature to automate attendance logging. 3. Automate the computation of employee rendered hours, deductions, and wages. 4. Provide administrators with a reporting feature to monitor employee attendance and generate payroll reports. 5. Test and evaluate the system's functionality, accuracy, and usability based on ISO 25010 quality standards."

[182] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] HOW TO BUILD YOUR OWN IPO DIAGRAM
[183] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] General Structure
[184] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] An IPO diagram has three connected boxes read left to right (or top to bottom): Input → Process → Output. Each box is filled with short bullet points, not full sentences.
[185] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Box 1: Input
[186] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] List everything the system needs to receive before it can do anything — raw data, user actions, or existing records.
[187] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Data entered by users (e.g., employee records, login credentials).
[188] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Data scanned or captured automatically (e.g., QR code scans, sensor data).
[189] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Existing reference data the system depends on (e.g., pay rates, schedules).
[190] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000'] TIP: If an item on your list doesn't come FROM outside the system, it doesn't belong in Input — it belongs in Process.
[191] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Box 2: Process
[192] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] List what the system DOES to the inputs — the actions, computations, or transformations performed internally.
[193] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Use verbs: validate, compute, encrypt, sort, generate, store, match.
[194] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Match each process item to a feature from your Scope of the Study — every listed feature should have a corresponding process step.
[195] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Keep each item short (one phrase); this is a summary diagram, not the full methodology.
[196] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Box 3: Output
[197] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] List what the system produces or displays as a result — the tangible deliverables the user receives.
[198] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000'] Reports, records, or documents generated (e.g., DTR, payslip).
[199] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Views or dashboards presented to the user.
[200] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Any decision or status the system determines (e.g., 'approved/denied,' 'present/absent').
[201] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000'] TIP: Every Output item should trace back to an Input + Process pairing — if you can't explain how an output was produced, the diagram has a gap.
[202] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Optional: Feedback Loop
[203] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Some studies add a dashed arrow from Output back to Input, representing continuous improvement (e.g., admin corrections feeding back into the system, or evaluation results informing future updates). Only include this if your study actually describes such a cycle.
[204] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Quick Checklist
[205] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Each box uses short bullet points, not paragraphs.
[206] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Input items are things that come INTO the system from outside.
[207] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Process items are actions the system performs, matching your listed features/modules.
[208] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Output items are what the user actually receives or sees.
[209] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] The diagram is followed by 1-2 short paragraphs in the text explaining the flow, for readers who want detail.

[212] [p] colors=[] Definition of Terms 
[213] [p] colors=[] 
[214] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000'] The Definition of Terms section is a standard part of a capstone project (usually placed at the end of Chapter 1 or in a preliminary section) that clarifies how key words, technical concepts, and jargon are used specifically within your study. It ensures readers interpret your terminology the same way you intend, especially when a word could have multiple meanings or is unfamiliar to a general audience.
[215] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Purpose
[216] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000'] Clarity – prevents misinterpretation of specialized, technical, or field-specific terms.
[217] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000'] Consistency – ensures the term is used the same way throughout the paper.
[218] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000'] Accessibility – helps readers outside your field (e.g., panel members, future researchers) understand your work.
[219] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000'] Precision – shows you understand the theoretical or conceptual grounding of your study.
[220] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] What to Include
[221] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000'] Technical/field-specific terms — words unique to your discipline (e.g., "machine learning," "phenomenology," "net present value").
[222] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000'] Variables from your study — especially independent/dependent variables, since these need operational definitions.
[223] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000'] Acronyms and abbreviations — spelled out and explained.
[224] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000'] Terms used in a specialized or non-standard way — words that have a different everyday meaning versus how you use them in the study.
[225] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000'] Locally or contextually specific terms — e.g., institution-specific programs, local terminology.
[226] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Two Common Types of Definitions
[227] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000'] Conceptual (Theoretical) Definition – the term's meaning based on literature, dictionaries, or established theory.
[228] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000'] Operational Definition – how the term is specifically measured, applied, or used within your study (most capstones require this, especially for variables).
[229] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000'] Formatting Guidelines
[230] [p] colors=['EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000', 'EE0000'] List terms alphabetically.

[251] [p] colors=['000000', '000000'] CHAPTER II
[252] [p] colors=['000000', '000000'] REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE AND STUDIES
[253] [p] colors=['000000', '000000'] This chapter presents the related literature and studies relevant to the system. It includes both local and foreign sources that support the development of the study and provide background information.
[254] [p] colors=['000000', '000000'] Digital Record-Keeping in Local Government Units
[255] [p] colors=['000000', '000000', '000000', '000000'] The digitalization of records in local government units (LGUs) has become an increasingly important response to the inefficiencies of manual, paper-based administration. Barangay offices, as the smallest units of local governance, traditionally rely on handwritten logbooks and physical filing systems for maintaining resident records, blotter reports, and certifications, a practice widely recognized as slow, error-prone, and vulnerable to loss or damage (Baycongan & Cruz, 2022; Villaruel et al., 2021). In response, various computerized and web-based record-management systems have been developed to automate data storage, retrieval, and reporting processes within barangay and municipal offices (Domingo et al., 2023; Fernandez & Aquino, 2022).
[256] [p] colors=['000000', '000000', '000000', '000000'] Empirical studies show that digitized record-keeping significantly improves administrative efficiency. Systems that centralize resident and household data allow barangay personnel to retrieve records within seconds rather than searching through physical files, reducing processing time for common transactions such as certificate requests and clearance issuance (Domingo et al., 2023; Bayongan & Cruz, 2022). Similarly, cloud-based recordkeeping platforms enable data backup and remote access, minimizing the risk of permanent data loss during calamities or office relocation (Fernandez & Aquino, 2022; Villaruel et al., 2021).
[257] [p] colors=['000000', '000000', '000000', '000000', '000000'] However, despite these advancements, many existing barangay record-keeping systems remain narrow in scope. Several are designed only for a single function, such as resident registration or blotter recording, without integration into a unified information system (Domingo et al., 2023; Fernandez & Aquino, 2022). Moreover, a number of these systems remain at the pilot or prototype stage, implemented in only one or two barangays, limiting evidence of their scalability to larger or more diverse local government contexts (Villaruel et al., 2021; Bayongan & Cruz, 2022).
[258] [p] colors=['000000', '000000'] Resident Profiling and Certificate Issuance Systems
[259] [p] colors=['000000', '000000'] Accurate resident profiling forms the foundation of effective barangay governance, as it supports service delivery, planning, and the issuance of official documents such as barangay clearances, indigency certificates, and business permits. Digital profiling systems aim to replace manual household surveys and card-based filing with structured electronic databases that capture demographic, health, and livelihood information (Ramos et al., 2022; Adeyemi & Okoro, 2021).
[260] [p] colors=['000000', '000000', '000000', '000000'] Research demonstrates that automated certificate issuance modules substantially reduce transaction time and human error. Systems that generate clearances and permits directly from a resident database eliminate repetitive manual encoding, allowing barangay staff to process requests more quickly and consistently (Ramos et al., 2022; Santos & Villamor, 2023). In addition, profiling systems integrated with reporting dashboards allow barangay officials to generate demographic summaries for use in local planning and disaster preparedness (Adeyemi & Okoro, 2021; Santos & Villamor, 2023).
[261] [p] colors=['000000', '000000', '000000'] Nevertheless, the effectiveness of these systems is often constrained by data quality and adoption challenges. Studies note that inconsistent or outdated resident data, arising from irregular updating practices, undermines the reliability of profiling systems (Santos & Villamor, 2023; Ramos et al., 2022). Furthermore, several systems lack safeguards against duplicate or fraudulent certificate requests, exposing gaps in verification and accountability (Adeyemi & Okoro, 2021).
[262] [p] colors=['000000', '000000'] Community Communication and Notification Systems
[263] [p] colors=['000000', '000000'] Timely communication between barangay officials and residents is essential for disseminating public advisories, emergency alerts, and community announcements. Traditional methods such as public postings and door-to-door notices are often slow and limited in reach, prompting the development of digital notification platforms for local governance (Marasigan & Del Rosario, 2022; Chukwu et al., 2021).
[264] [p] colors=['000000', '000000'] Studies indicate that SMS-based and mobile application notification systems considerably improve the speed and reach of barangay communication. Platforms that automatically send alerts for community events, health advisories, or emergency situations enable residents to receive information in real time, improving public safety and civic participation (Marasigan & Del Rosario, 2022; Chukwu et al., 2021). Web-based announcement boards further support transparency by allowing residents to view ongoing projects, meeting schedules, and public notices at any time (Chukwu et al., 2021; Marasigan & Del Rosario, 2022).
[265] [p] colors=['000000', '000000', '000000'] However, these systems face adoption barriers similar to those found in other local-government platforms. Limited internet connectivity and low smartphone penetration in some barangays restrict the reach of digital notifications, particularly among older residents (Chukwu et al., 2021; Marasigan & Del Rosario, 2022). Additionally, many notification systems function only as one-way broadcast tools, offering little to no mechanism for residents to respond, request assistance, or report concerns back to barangay officials.
[266] [p] colors=['000000', '000000'] Data Security and Access Control in Government Information Systems
[267] [p] colors=['000000', '000000'] As barangay information systems increasingly store sensitive resident data, security and access control have become critical design considerations. Government information systems are expected to comply with data privacy regulations while remaining accessible to authorized personnel across different administrative roles (Nolasco et al., 2023; Owusu & Mensah, 2022).
[268] [p] colors=['000000', '000000'] Research highlights those role-based access control mechanisms, which restrict system functions according to user privileges, significantly reduce the risk of unauthorized data modification or disclosure (Nolasco et al., 2023; Owusu & Mensah, 2022). Encrypted data storage and audit-trail logging further strengthen accountability by allowing administrators to track changes made within the system (Owusu & Mensah, 2022).
[269] [p] colors=['000000', '000000'] Despite these safeguards, several studies report that smaller local-government systems often implement only minimal security measures due to limited technical expertise and budget constraints (Nolasco et al., 2023). This leaves resident data vulnerable to unauthorized access, particularly in systems that lack proper authentication protocols or regular security audits (Owusu & Mensah, 2022; Nolasco et al., 2023).
[270] [p] colors=['000000', '000000'] System Limitations and Usability Challenges in Existing Platforms
[271] [p] colors=['000000', '000000'] Despite the growing number of digital systems developed for barangay governance, several recurring limitations are evident across existing studies. A prominent issue is the lack of integration between core functions such as resident profiling, certificate issuance, and community communication, resulting in fragmented systems that require separate logins or platforms for each task (Domingo et al., 2023; Marasigan & Del Rosario, 2022).
[272] [p] colors=['000000', '000000'] Another significant limitation is limited two-way engagement between residents and barangay officials. Most existing platforms function primarily as administrative tools for barangay staff, offering residents little to no ability to track their own requests, submit concerns, or receive personalized updates (Ramos et al., 2022; Chukwu et al., 2021).
[273] [p] colors=['000000', '000000'] In addition, many systems provide minimal reporting and analytics capabilities, limiting officials' ability to generate demographic insights or monitor service-delivery trends over time (Adeyemi & Okoro, 2021; Santos & Villamor, 2023). Issues of scalability and security also persist, as several platforms remain confined to single-barangay pilot deployments with limited safeguards for resident data (Nolasco et al., 2023; Villaruel et al., 2021).
[274] [p] colors=['000000', '000000'] Synthesis of the Literature and Research Gap
[275] [p] colors=['000000', '000000', '000000', '000000', '000000'] The reviewed literature highlights the expanding role of digital systems in improving barangay governance, resident service delivery, and community communication. Existing platforms have demonstrated meaningful contributions in reducing processing time, improving data accessibility, and strengthening transparency by replacing manual, paper-based processes with centralized digital solutions (Domingo et al., 2023; Bayongan & Cruz, 2022).
[276] [p] colors=['000000', '000000'] However, consistent limitations remain across these systems. Many platforms operate within narrow functional scopes, addressing isolated components such as resident profiling or certificate issuance without integrating them into a comprehensive system (Domingo et al., 2023; Ramos et al., 2022). The lack of two-way communication channels further limits resident engagement, while inconsistent security practices leave sensitive data vulnerable in several implementations (Chukwu et al., 2021; Nolasco et al., 2023). Additionally, several systems emphasize administrative processing over analytical insight, offering limited reporting features that could otherwise support local planning and decision-making (Adeyemi & Okoro, 2021; Santos & Villamor, 2023).
[277] [p] colors=['000000', '000000'] These gaps highlight the need for a more holistic and resident-centered approach to barangay information management. Specifically, there is limited development of platforms that integrate resident profiling, certificate issuance, community notification, and secure data management into a single, accessible system.
[278] [p] colors=['000000', '000000', '000000'] Therefore, this study addresses these limitations by developing the Barangay Information and Management System (BIMS), a web-based platform designed to provide a comprehensive, secure, and user-friendly solution for barangay record-keeping and community service delivery. The system aims to streamline administrative processes, strengthen resident engagement, and support data-driven local governance by integrating key barangay functions into a unified platform.
