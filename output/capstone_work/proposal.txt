[9] [p] colors=['EE0000'] Cited Problems 
[10] [p] colors=[] This study seeks to answer the following problems:
[11] [p] colors=[] Manual parcel verification, inventory deduction, and packing-material monitoring increase the risk of stock discrepancies, shipment delays, and incomplete shipment documentation, which negatively affects warehouse efficiency and order fulfillment.
[12] [p] colors=[] Inventory is recorded manually using paper-based records and disconnected spreadsheets. Employees frequently encounter erasures, inconsistent stock entries, and miscalculations during counting. Recorded inventory often does not match actual stock, leading to overcounting, undercounting, and inefficient monitoring across raw materials, packaging, supplies, and finished goods.
[13] [p] colors=[] The absence of an automated costing mechanism may result in inaccurate production cost computation, inconsistent packaging expense tracking, and unreliable selling-price decisions, affecting financial decision-making and business profitability.
[14] [p] colors=[] Because orders from Shopee, VIP (Website and Facebook orders), and other channels are manually organized and waybills are manually matched to order lists, employees spend significant time verifying parcel contents. This increases packing errors, shipment delays, and inefficient fulfillment workflows.
[15] [p] colors=[] Manual recording of retail and walk-in transactions delays sales processing and inventory updates, resulting in unorganized sales records, delayed stock deductions, and inefficient store operations.
[16] [p] colors=[] The absence of an automated RTS process makes it difficult to track returned or cancelled parcels, restore sellable inventory accurately, and maintain reliable return history, leading to stock inconsistencies and inefficient return handling.
[17] [p] colors=['EE0000'] Functions of the Proposed System
[18] [p] colors=[] The proposed Pyx Tracker system functions as a centralized, role-based web platform that unifies inventory, production, multi-channel fulfillment, retail sales, and returns management for Pyx Food Products. It consolidates honey and department production workflows from warehouse stock-in through bottling and pouch finishing alongside packaging-material and supplies tracking with role-appropriate stock-in, deduct, and approval actions. The platform processes online orders through VIP and Shopee SPX preparation tools, PDF and Excel waybill stamping, batch finalization, and parcel records linked to a deliveries module where staff scan tracking numbers, capture before-shipping photos, deduct finished goods and packing materials, and update parcel status from To Pack to Shipped. An executive-only costing hub computes manufacturing COGS, parcel packaging tiers, and selling prices. The integrated POS module handles walk-in honey and department product sales with real-time inventory deduction, receipts, and accounts-receivable credits. The RTS module records return cases, syncs sellable and damaged inventory pools, and supports Use RTS deductions for POS and packaging. Dashboards, sales reports, supplier records, and staff management provide oversight for executives and production managers.

[20] [p] colors=[] The proposed title includes key features aligned with the cited problems:
[21] [p] colors=[] Parcel Processing and Inventory Synchronization

[23] [p] colors=[] Integrated Inventory Management System
[24] [p] colors=[] Centralizes raw materials, packaging materials, supplies, and finished goods; tracks the honey pipeline (warehouse to production to packaging) and department production (Paragis Tea, BRC, Jar Cacao); records stock movements, and maintains real-time on-hand balances across warehouse, online, and retail channels.
[25] [p] colors=[] Product Costing and Pricing Calculator
[26] [p] colors=[] Automatically computes manufacturing production costs, fulfillment packaging expenses, and selling prices with separate formulas for product COGS and parcel packaging tiers to support consistent and accurate pricing decisions.

[28] [p] colors=[] Centralizes orders from multiple selling platforms (including VIP and Shopee SPX), organizes them for fulfillment through batch workflows, and automates waybill matching, stamping, and master Excel generation to improve efficiency and accuracy.
[29] [p] colors=[] Point of Sale (POS) System
[30] [p] colors=[] Digitizes walk-in retail transactions through PIN-based POS login, automatically records sales, deducts inventory (including RTS vs. non-RTS stock source selection), generates receipts, and maintains transaction and sales history.
[31] [p] colors=[] Return-to-Sender (RTS) Management
[32] [p] colors=[] Uses tracking-number lookup and barcode scanning to process returned or cancelled parcels, record RTS cases with line items, sync sellable and damaged inventory pools, restore stock automatically, and maintain return history and dashboard counts.

[75] [p] colors=[] REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE AND STUDIES
[76] [p] colors=[] This chapter presents related literature and studies relevant to the development of Pyx Tracker: Web-based Enterprise Operations Management System.
[77] [p] colors=[] Web-Based Inventory and Production Management for Food SMEs
[78] [p] colors=[] Digitizing inventory management has become a common response to the limits of paper forms and spreadsheet-based tracking in small and medium enterprises (SMEs), especially in the Philippines where branch-level stock requests and manual sales reporting still appear in daily operations. Tanaman et al. (2023) developed a web-based inventory management system for a multi-branch enterprise in Pagadian City and reported that manual paper forms led to data inaccuracy, lost records, and slow sales-report preparation; their web platform addressed these issues by recording and processing inventory electronically. Similarly, Lorzano et al. (2022) designed a web-based inventory system for True North Empanada, a food retail company, after finding that weekly manual consolidation in Excel caused discrepancies between actual and recorded stock and delayed raw-ingredient ordering. Laping et al. (2023) applied a design-thinking approach to inventory management for perishable goods in a convenience store in Marikina City, emphasizing that perishable inventory requires timely, user-focused digital tools because spoilage increases when stock is not tracked accurately.
[79] [p] colors=[] Foreign literature likewise treats integrated digital inventory as a baseline requirement for operational control. Goedhart et al. (2023) modeled replenishment and fulfillment across an omni-channel retail network and showed that coordinated inventory decisions across stores and channels improve service levels and reduce imbalanced stock across locations. Bhatti and Bauirzhanovna (2023) further noted that intelligent inventory systems strengthen warehousing activity when products move through complex forward and reverse flows.
[80] [p] colors=[] However, many published local inventory systems focus on a single branch type retail stores, convenience outlets, or empanada commissaries without combining production pipelines (raw materials to WIP to finished goods), packaging-material pools, and multi-channel sales deductions in one platform. Existing prototypes also rarely document role-based workflows for HR stock-in, production deduct, and executive correction in the same system, leaving a gap that Pyx Tracker intends to fill for a food-manufacturing SME.
[81] [p] colors=[] Parcel Processing, Barcode Scanning, and Shipment Verification
[82] [p] colors=[] Automatic identification technologies play a central role in verifying shipments before dispatch and in reducing manual encoding during consolidation. Chocholáč et al. (2022) compared barcode and RFID technologies in a shipment-consolidation laboratory experiment and found measurable time savings when scans replace fully manual identification steps in last-mile logistics. Kamnardsiri et al. (2022) introduced the ParcelBar dataset for parcel-barcode recognition and demonstrated that deep-learning models can reliably detect barcodes on post-box labels in warehouse settings, supporting the feasibility of scan-driven parcel workflows. GS1 US (2026) reported that two-dimensional barcodes including QR codes and Data Matrix can encode product, lot, and shipment attributes in a single scan, reducing relabeling, mis-shipments, and exception handling in distribution operations.
[83] [p] colors=[] Local capstone and industry-oriented studies increasingly pair scanning with inventory updates. Ercilla et al. (2024) proposed an inventory and POS system for Bida Mart that identified future barcode integration as a needed enhancement for faster stock verification at the point of sale. Operational practice at food and wellness SMEs that ship through Shopee, VIP, and courier partners depends on matching tracking numbers to packed items a process that scan-based verification can speed up the process when tied directly to inventory deduction.
[84] [p] colors=[] However, few documented systems in the local literature combine parcel scan verification, automatic finished-goods deduction, packing-material deduction, before-shipping photo capture, and status updates from To Pack to Shipped in one deliveries module. Most studies stop at identification speed or POS barcode prototypes rather than full fulfillment-to-inventory synchronization, which remains an open design target for Pyx Tracker.
[85] [p] colors=[] Product Costing and Pricing Information Systems
[86] [p] colors=[] Accurate production costing supports pricing decisions for food-industry SMEs that still rely on informal or manual calculations. Munifah et al. (2026) developed a web-based cost-of-goods-manufactured information system using the variable-costing method for UD Karya Abadi, a snack-food MSME, and found that manual bookkeeping produced inaccurate cost figures and weak selling-price decisions; the web system integrated raw materials, labor, operating expenses, and production data into structured COGM computation. Local accounting-oriented studies consistently report that SMEs under-record overhead and packaging costs when spreadsheets replace formal formulas, leading to prices that do not reflect true production expense.
[87] [p] colors=[] From a managerial perspective, separating manufacturing COGS from fulfillment packaging cost matters for businesses that both produce goods and ship parcels with bubble wrap, pouches, and cushion materials. Digital costing tools that store formula lines, supplier prices, and tier-based parcel recipes allow executives to explain price changes from auditable data rather than ad-hoc estimates a design principle reflected in Pyx Tracker's executive-only costing hub.
[88] [p] colors=[] However, existing local costing systems often cover production formulas alone and do not link costing outputs to live inventory prices, parcel-ship packaging tiers, and POS selling prices within the same operations platform. Foreign ERP literature discusses enterprise-wide costing, but SME-focused local studies seldom integrate costing with fulfillment and retail modules, leaving Pyx Food Products without a single tool that connects factory cost, ship cost, and shelf price.
[89] [p] colors=[] Multi-Platform Order Processing and Waybill Management
[90] [p] colors=[] Multi-channel selling increases order volume but also fragments order data across marketplaces, spreadsheets, and courier waybill PDFs. Goedhart et al. (2023) showed that retailers serving both in-store and online demand need fulfillment policies that allocate orders using store-level inventory rather than myopic, one-day rules that create stock imbalances. Nanayakkara et al. (2022) emphasized that rising e-commerce return volumes make forward fulfillment and reverse flows interdependent; centralized order and return data become necessary for sustainable logistics planning.
[91] [p] colors=[] In the Philippine context, SMEs often sell through Shopee and custom VIP channels while preparing courier waybills manually. Divina et al. (2025) addressed manual sales and inventory pain in a small food enterprise by unifying transaction processing and stock tracking in one web system, illustrating the productivity gain when disconnected logs are replaced by a single platform though their scope was restaurant POS rather than marketplace prep, Excel merge tools, and PDF waybill stamping.
[92] [p] colors=[] However, local studies rarely document VIP Excel preparation, Shopee SPX packing-list import, unified PDF-and-Excel waybill stamping, and batch finalization that creates parcel records as one CSR workflow. Tools that handle only one marketplace or only inventory not order prep plus waybill generation leave employees switching between scripts and files, which is the operational gap Pyx Tracker's Online Orders module targets.
[93] [p] colors=[] Point-of-Sale Systems and Integrated Retail Transactions
[94] [p] colors=[] Point-of-sale systems that share a database with inventory reduce double encoding and improve the reliability of on-hand counts. Ercilla et al. (2024) reported that a proposed inventory and POS system for Bida Mart could provide real-time stock visibility, replenishment alerts, and sales reports that manual tracking could not support. Divina et al. (2025) evaluated a web-based POS and inventory system for Pastil sa Tabi using ISO/IEC 25010 criteria and found high usability and functionality ratings; respondents noted faster operations and more reliable inventory updates when sales and stock were integrated. Lorzano et al. (2022) likewise linked POS sales to bill-of-materials computation so raw-ingredient status updates immediately after each product sale in a food retail setting.
[95] [p] colors=[] Integrated POS matters for businesses that sell both online and walk-in, especially when returned stock (RTS pool) must be selectable at checkout. Divina et al. (2025) noted that small food enterprises gain the most from POS when sales, inventory, and analytics share one platform rather than operating as separate tools.
[96] [p] colors=[] However, many local POS implementations target single-site food retail or sari-sari stores and do not support PIN-based POS roles, department product catalogs (e.g., honey variants, Paragis, BRC, Jar Cacao), accounts-receivable staff credits, or RTS vs. non-RTS stock source selection. Pyx Tracker extends POS integration into a wider operations hub rather than a standalone store terminal.
[97] [p] colors=[] Return-to-Sender Management and Reverse Logistics
[98] [p] colors=[] Product returns and courier RTS parcels create reverse flows that disrupt inventory accuracy when restocking is handled manually. Nanayakkara et al. (2022) proposed a three-stage circular reverse-logistics framework for e-commerce returns and argued that clustering return patterns and optimizing recovery networks reduces cost and waste compared with ad-hoc return handling. Bhatti and Bauirzhanovna (2023) found through expert interviews that intelligent inventory systems improve reverse-logistics warehousing and resource replacement when returns re-enter the supply chain. Chocholáč et al. (2022) noted that identification technology at consolidation points affects how quickly returned or re-routed shipments are processed in distribution logistics.
[99] [p] colors=[] Locally, RTS handling for wellness and food parcels is often tracked on paper case logs, with sellable returns manually merged into stock counts. Without a synced sellable pool and audit trail for Use RTS deductions at POS or packaging, businesses risk double-counting or losing returned goods.
[100] [p] colors=[] However, published local capstone systems seldom combine RTS case recording, tracking-number prefill from deliveries, sellable and damaged pools, inventory sync, and barcode-assisted restocking in one module connected to forward fulfillment. Most reverse-logistics literature remains foreign and theoretical at network level, not implemented as a practical web module for a Philippine SME another gap Pyx Tracker addresses.
[101] [p] colors=[] Synthesis of the Literature and Identification of the Research Gap
[102] [p] colors=[] The reviewed literature and studies agree that web-based platforms improve inventory accuracy, speed up order and sales processing, and reduce manual errors for SMEs in the Philippines and abroad (Tanaman et al., 2023; Lorzano et al., 2022; Divina et al., 2025; Ercilla et al., 2024). Barcode and two-dimensional code technologies support faster parcel identification and fewer shipment exceptions (Chocholáč et al., 2022; Kamnardsiri et al., 2022; GS1 US, 2026). Costing information systems help food-industry MSMEs move from manual HPP/COGM estimates to structured, repeatable calculations (Munifah et al., 2026). Multi-channel and omni-channel research shows that fragmented order and inventory decisions create stock imbalance and fulfillment delay (Goedhart et al., 2023; Nanayakkara et al., 2022). POS–inventory integration and reverse-logistics tooling further show that forward and return flows must share one data source to stay reliable (Bhatti & Bauirzhanovna, 2023; Divina et al., 2025).
[103] [p] colors=[] Despite these contributions, consistent gaps appear across the literature. First, local systems often solve one function inventory, POS, or costing without unifying production, packaging materials, supplies, and finished goods in a single role-based platform. Second, parcel scan-to-ship workflows that deduct both product and packing materials and capture shipment photos are underrepresented in local empirical studies. Third, multi-platform CSR fulfillment (marketplace prep, waybill stamping, batch parcels) is rarely documented as an integrated module. Fourth, RTS management with synced sellable inventory and POS consumption of return stock lacks local implementation examples tied to real courier operations. Fifth, few studies target food-manufacturing SMEs that combine in-house production (honey and department lines), online marketplace sales, walk-in POS, and courier-based fulfillment in one organization.
[104] [p] colors=[] Therefore, this study addresses these limitations by developing Pyx Tracker: Web-based Enterprise Operations Management System. a centralized system for Pyx Food Products that integrates parcel processing and inventory synchronization, production-aware inventory management, product costing and pricing, multi-platform order and waybill tools, POS, and RTS management within a single web application with role-appropriate access for executives, production managers, HR, CSR staff, warehouse personnel, and cashiers.
